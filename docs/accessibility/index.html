Accessibility

OoidOS treats accessibility as a requirement. The target is WCAG 2.2 at level AA for the whole desktop and its apps, in every look, light and dark.

Tested, not eyeballed

Every pair of colours WCAG cares about is named by its role, not by its value, and checked by a test in every look, light and dark: text at least 4.5:1 against its background, text on coloured controls 4.5:1, large text 3:1, and what identifies a control or its state (field borders, the focus ring, scroll bars) 3:1. A new look, such as Redmond, is held to the same pairs as soon as its colours are added. An audit of the desktop and its apps against WCAG 2.2 AA is kept with the code.

Settings › Accessibility

The pane groups its settings by what they help with: Vision, Hearing and Motor. Every change applies at once, to the desktop and every app.

SettingWhat it does
VoiceOverTurns the screen reader on and off. See Screen reader.
ZoomMagnifies the screen from 1 to 20 times, following the pointer (continuously, at the edges, or centred) and the window you are typing in.
Increase contrastStrengthens every colour by its role: text 7:1, text on controls 4.5:1, every control, border and separator 3:1. It also reduces transparency.
Reduce transparencyMakes the menu bar, menus, dock, sidebars and other glass surfaces opaque.
Differentiate without colourAdds a shape to anything shown by colour alone, such as a status dot.
Reduce motionWindows, Panorama, tiling and switching desktops fade in place; the dock stops magnifying and shows and hides at once.
Text sizeFrom smaller than default up to twice the size, in every app; layouts reflow to fit.
PointerIts size (up to four times), and its outline and fill colours.
Invert colours, colour filtersGreyscale, filters for protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia, a tint, and their intensity.
Sticky KeysA modifier key pressed and let go applies to the next key; pressed twice, it locks.
Slow KeysA key counts only once it has been held for a set time.
Mouse KeysThe numeric keypad moves the pointer and clicks.
Double-click speedHow quickly two clicks must follow each other.
Flash the screenFlashes the screen when an alert sound plays.
Mono audioPlays stereo sound as mono on both sides.

Full Keyboard Access, which lets you move between every control with the keyboard, has its switch in Settings › Keyboard, and the Accessibility pane links to it.

Keys

KeysWhat they do
⌥ ❖ 8Turn Zoom on or off
⌥ ❖ = / ⌥ ❖ -Zoom in / out
Ctrl ⌥ ❖ 8Invert colours
❖ F5Turn VoiceOver on or off
❖ F2 or Ctrl F2Move to the menu bar, to work its menus with the arrow keys

❖ is OoidOS’s Command key, the one many keyboards mark ❖. More in Keyboard shortcuts.

Screen reader

OoidOS’s apps draw themselves with Ooidget, so no toolkit underneath tells a screen reader what is on screen. Instead, each of the kit’s components describes itself (its role, name, states and actions), and an app’s tree of these descriptions is published through AT-SPI, the accessibility interface Linux screen readers use. Calculator is the first app read this way.

VoiceOver in Settings, and ❖ F5, start and stop Orca, the GNOME project’s screen reader.

Orca is coming to the system image next, with the speech services it needs. Until then, the VoiceOver switch has no screen reader to start.

Still to come

  • Orca and its speech services in the image.
  • The screen reader proper: every component and every app described, and the desktop’s own menu bar, dock and menus.
  • Window buttons with larger targets (24 pixels, WCAG 2.5.8), keeping their look.
  • Text size for the menu bar, dock and menus.
  • Outlines for checkboxes, radio buttons and switches, and Cupertino’s focus ring and accent text at AA by default.
