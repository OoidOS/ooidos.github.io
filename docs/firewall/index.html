The firewall (Gatehouse)

Gatehouse is OoidOS’s firewall. It’s on from the moment your computer starts. It checks what comes in, and if you want, it can also check what your apps send out.

Incoming connections

Gatehouse blocks connections nobody asked for. The only exceptions are the services you’ve turned on, and their ports are only open while they’re running: SSH while Remote Login is on, VNC while Screen Sharing is on, SMB while File Sharing is on, and Toss’s port while receiving is on. You’ll find its settings in Settings › Network › Firewall.

Outgoing connections

Gatehouse can also keep an eye on what apps send out. It has four modes:

ModeWhat happens when an app connects somewhere new
OffNothing is checked
AlertYou’re asked
Silent AllowIt’s allowed, and noted down
Silent DenyIt’s blocked, and noted down

An alert shows the app’s icon and name, and where it wants to connect. You can allow or deny it for just that server, its whole domain, or anywhere, and either until the app quits or for good. If you don’t answer an alert in time, it turns into a notification.

The Gatehouse app

The Gatehouse app is in Applications › Utilities. It shows the live connections for each app (their hosts, ports, data in and out, and whether they were allowed or denied), your rules (search, add, edit or remove them), the current mode and the traffic. You can also open it from the firewall item in the menu bar.

From the command line

The gatehouse command does the same from a terminal. It comes with a man page and completions for bash, zsh and fish. Type man gatehouse to read it.
