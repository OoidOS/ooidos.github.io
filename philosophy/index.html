Philosophy

What OoidOS stands for

A computer should feel calm. Apps should be easy to add and easy to remove. Your windows should be where you left them, your files should be safe, and nothing should leave your computer unless you sent it.

OoidOS is built around those ideas. Here’s what that means in practice.

Apps you can hold

An app is a single item. Drag it into Applications to install it. Drag it to the Bin to remove it, and everything it installed goes with it. Apps come as a single .ooid file.

The tools you use every day have clear names: Panorama shows all your windows, Toss sends files to devices nearby, Rewind keeps your backups, Gatehouse is the firewall, and Scribe is for writing.

Everything looks like it belongs together

The window frames, the menu bar, the Dock, the notifications and every app look and work the same way, so there’s less to learn. A look changes all of them at once. OoidOS’s own look is warm and earthy, Cupertino is cooler and crisper, and more looks are on the way.

Updates you can trust

An update goes in next to the system you’re using, never over it. If the new version doesn’t start properly, your computer goes back to the one that worked. And if something does go wrong, Recovery is always there to repair or reinstall OoidOS.

For everyone

Accessibility is part of the design, not an add-on. OoidOS aims for WCAG 2.2 AA everywhere, checks contrast in every look, light and dark, and can be used with the keyboard alone. Settings › Accessibility brings together Zoom, Increase contrast, Reduce motion, larger text and much more.

Any computer

OoidOS is made for all kinds of PCs and laptops. It works with whatever hardware it finds, so there’s no list of models to check. Today it runs on 64-bit Intel and AMD processors, with ARM on the roadmap.

Private by default

OoidOS sends nothing about you anywhere: no tracking, no account, not even in the Store. Gatehouse blocks connections nobody asked for. It can show you every connection your apps make, and ask you before a new one. Sharing your screen, your files or a terminal stays off until you turn it on.

Every piece accounted for

OoidOS is built from source code by its own build tool. Every source is pinned to an exact checksum, so each build uses exactly what the last one did, and you can always tell where any part of the system came from. Curious? You can build it yourself.

Straight with you

OoidOS is in alpha. We say what works and what’s coming, and we keep the roadmap up to date.
